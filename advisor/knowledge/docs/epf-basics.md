# Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) basics

## What EPF is
The Employees' Provident Fund is a retirement savings scheme run by the EPFO for salaried employees in covered organisations. Part of your salary goes into your EPF account every month and your employer adds a matching contribution.

## How much goes in
The standard contribution is 12% of basic salary plus dearness allowance from the employee. The employer also contributes 12%, but a part of the employer share goes to the pension scheme (EPS) instead of the EPF balance.

## Interest
EPFO declares the interest rate each year. Interest is credited to your account and compounds over the years, which is why leaving the money invested helps long-term savings.

## Withdrawing money
You can usually withdraw fully at retirement. Partial withdrawals are allowed for specific reasons such as medical needs, marriage, education or buying a home, subject to EPFO rules. Withdrawing when you change jobs reduces your retirement savings, so consider transferring the account to the new employer instead.

## Useful tools
Your UAN (Universal Account Number) lets you check your balance, passbook and transfers on the EPFO member portal. Check the current rules on the official website.
