# Credit cards and the minimum due trap

## How a credit card works
A credit card lets you spend now and pay later. If you pay the full statement amount by the due date, you usually pay no interest on purchases.

## The minimum due trap
The minimum due is a small part of your bill. If you pay only the minimum, the rest of the balance becomes a loan on which interest is charged, and card interest rates are usually much higher than home or personal loan rates. The unpaid balance can keep growing, and you also lose the interest-free period on new purchases.

## Other costs
Late payment fees, cash-withdrawal charges, foreign-currency mark-up and annual fees can add up. Read the card's charges list before using it.

## Safe habits
Pay the full amount every month, set up auto-pay, keep your spending well below the limit, and never share your card number, CVV or OTP with anyone. If you cannot pay in full, pay as much as you can above the minimum and avoid new card spending until the balance is cleared.
