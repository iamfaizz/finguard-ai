# The 50/30/20 budgeting rule and emergency funds

## What the rule says
The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to split your monthly take-home income: about 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and debt repayment. It is a starting point, not a law.

## Needs (about 50%)
Costs you cannot easily skip: rent or home-loan EMI, groceries, electricity and other utilities, transport to work or college, insurance premiums and minimum loan payments.

## Wants (about 30%)
Things you enjoy but could cut if money is tight: eating out, food delivery, shopping, streaming subscriptions, travel and entertainment.

## Savings and debt repayment (about 20%)
Building an emergency fund, SIP investments, PPF or EPF contributions and extra loan repayments.

## Adjusting the rule in India
In expensive cities rent can push needs above 50%. If so, trim wants first and keep savings steady, even if the share is smaller. Students with low income can start with 10% savings and increase it as income grows.

## Emergency fund
Many financial educators suggest keeping three to six months of essential expenses aside. Keep it somewhere safe and easy to withdraw from, such as a savings account or a liquid fund, not in risky investments. Build it before taking on new investments.
