# Public Provident Fund (PPF) basics

## What PPF is
The Public Provident Fund is a long-term savings scheme backed by the Government of India. You can open an account at a post office or an authorised bank.

## How it works
The account runs for 15 years and can be extended in blocks of five years. The government sets the interest rate and reviews it every quarter, so check the current rate before investing. There is a minimum and a maximum amount you can deposit in a financial year, and the maximum is Rs 1.5 lakh.

## Withdrawals and loans
Money is locked in for a long time. Partial withdrawals are allowed only after a few years, and loans against the balance are allowed in the early years. Check the current rules before planning to use the money.

## Tax treatment
Deposits can qualify for a deduction under Section 80C if you choose the old tax regime, and the interest is generally tax-free. Tax rules change, so check the as-of date and the official source.

## Who it suits
People who want a safe, long-term, low-risk part of their savings and do not need the money for many years.
